# snowcave: surviving a blizzard in a snow cave (Short 06, idea, no reference)

Voice first; word times in `snowcave_words.json`. Script shape: the problem chain of `research/format.md`.

Facts (checked 2026-11-09: US Army FM 31-70 excerpt, Mother Earth News, Wikipedia "Snow cave", outdoor manuals): snow is
mostly trapped air and insulates; a well-built snow cave stays around freezing, about 32 F, even at -40 F outside (reports,
not lab measurements). The entrance goes in low and rises to the chamber; cold air sinks and pools low, so the sleeping
platform is above the top of the entrance; a domed, smooth ceiling lets melt water run down the walls instead of dripping; a
small vent hole in the roof lets out carbon dioxide (manuals advise it; it must be kept clear); no open fire inside; one
candle is common.

## Narration

1. It's forty below, and in a few hours, this storm will kill you.
2. You can't outrun it. And the wind is what's freezing you.
3. So you dig straight into a snowdrift, and get out of the wind.
4. But the hole is a freezer, because cold air is pouring in the door.
5. So you dig the door low, and you build your bed up high.
6. Cold air is heavy. It sinks, and it stays in the tunnel, below you.
7. But your body heat melts the ceiling, and now it's raining on you.
8. So you carve the roof into a smooth dome, and the water runs down the walls.
9. Now you're warm and dry, and you start to feel sleepy.
10. That's the carbon dioxide. Every breath is filling this sealed room.
11. So you poke one small hole in the roof, and you keep it clear all night.
12. Outside, it's still forty below. In here, it's thirty-two degrees.
13. You just survived the night inside a pile of snow.

## Scene idea

Hook: whiteout, wind and snow, a tiny figure against a huge drift. Then the drift is sliced like a cake: white snow in section,
dark hollow inside, warm candle glow. Cold air is blue and heavy and settles in the tunnel, warm air orange and on top.
