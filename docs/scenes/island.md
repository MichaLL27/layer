# island: finding drinking water on a desert island (Short, idea, no reference)

Voice first; word times in `island_words.json`. Format: the problem chain of `research/format.md`
"What the 29M video does". Lazar's two rules for this one (2026-11-08): it opens on a hook that
stops the viewer, and everything we show must read clearly.

Facts: sea water is about 3.5% salt, more than kidneys can pass, so drinking it dehydrates you
faster; about three days without water is the usual survival figure. Rain soaks through island sand
and, being less dense than sea water (1000 vs 1025 kg/m3), floats on it as a "freshwater lens"
(Ghyben-Herzberg: about 40 m below sea level per 1 m above it). Near the shore the lens is thin, so
a deep hole reaches salt; pumping hard draws the salt up ("upconing"). US Army survival manual
FM 21-76: dig behind the first dune, line the hole. Small islands need enough rain to hold a lens,
and ground water should be boiled.

## Narration

1. Drink this water, and you die faster.
2. You're stranded on an island, and thirst will kill you in three days.
3. So you dig a hole on the beach, and it fills with water!
4. But it's salt. The sea soaks right through the sand.
5. So you walk inland, behind the dunes, and dig again.
6. This time, the water is fresh.
7. Because rain sinks into the sand, and fresh water is lighter than salt water, so it floats.
8. Right under your feet, a giant bubble of drinking water is sitting on top of the sea.
9. Every foot it rises above sea level, it pushes forty feet down.
10. But this close to the shore, it's thin. Dig too deep, and you punch into the salt.
11. So you stop the moment you see water, and you line the hole with driftwood so the sand can't cave in.
12. And you scoop slowly, because if you take too much, the salt rises to meet you.
13. Boil it, and you just bought yourself weeks. People have lived on tiny islands like this for thousands of years.

## Scene idea

The whole island is sliced in half like a cake: above the cut a beach, dunes and palms; on the cut
face plain sand, then the two waters in two clear colours. Test clip = sentences 1-8.
