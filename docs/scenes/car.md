# car — getting out of a car that goes into a river (Short 03)

Voice first; word times in `car_words.json`. Format: the problem chain of `research/format.md`
"What the 29M video does" (second person, "but... so...", named fixes, "Congratulations").
Reference for the format only: `research/ref_tV8D/` (You Made It, coffin escape, 69 s). Ours: own
topic, own telling, ~50 s (-27%).

Facts (the widely taught "seatbelt, window, children, out" order): a car usually floats for
roughly 30 s to 2 min; a phone call first wastes that time; water pressure holds the doors shut
while there is a difference in level (about half a ton on a door); power windows often work for a
short while after the car hits the water; tempered side glass shatters when struck at a corner with
a window breaker, the laminated windshield does not; a front-engine car tips nose down; once the
car is full the pressure equalises and a door can open (last resort).

## Narration

1. Your car just went off a bridge into a river. It floats for about a minute, then it sinks.
2. Your first thought is to call for help, but there's no time. So you leave the phone.
3. Seatbelt off first, or it holds you down when the water rises.
4. Then you push the door, but the river holds it shut with half a ton of force.
5. So you open the window while the power still works. This is your way out.
6. But the button dies. So you hit the corner of the side glass with a window breaker.
7. Never the windshield. It's laminated and won't break.
8. Now the river pours in, and the heavy engine tips the car nose down.
9. Kids go out first, through the window, then you.
10. And if nothing breaks? Wait until the car fills. Then the pressure evens out and the door opens.
11. Take a breath, push off, and swim toward the light.
12. Congratulations. You just got out of a sinking car.
