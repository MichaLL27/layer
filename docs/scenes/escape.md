# escape: the Great Escape tunnel "Harry" (Short 07, idea, no reference; topic picked by Lazar 2026-11-11)

Voice first; word times in `escape_words.json`. Script shape: the problem chain of `research/format.md`; the name comes last.

Facts (checked 2026-11-11: PBS NOVA "Great Escape", History.com, stalagluft3.com, GUARD Archaeology): Stalag Luft III, Sagan
(now Zagan, Poland). Seismograph microphones were buried along the fence, so Harry's shaft went down about 30 ft (9 m). The
soil is loose sand: the tunnel, about 2 ft square, was shored with bed boards (about 4,000 taken across Tom, Dick and Harry).
Air: a bellows pump worked like a rowing machine (kitbags, hockey sticks), pushing air through a pipe of milk tins laid in
the floor. The subsoil sand is bright yellow, the surface soil grey: it was carried out in bags hidden in trousers and
coats and shaken out in the gardens. Entrance under the stove of Hut 104. Harry was about 102 m (336 ft) long; it came up
past the fence but short of the trees, about 45 ft from a guard tower. 76 men got out on 24-25 March 1944; 3 reached home.

## Narration

1. You're locked in a prison camp, and the only way out is under the fence.
2. But the guards buried microphones that hear every shovel.
3. So you dig thirty feet straight down, too deep to be heard.
4. But this ground is pure sand, and the roof keeps falling in.
5. So you pull the boards out of your bed, and box in every foot.
6. But down here, there's no air, and your lamp goes out.
7. So you build a pump from kit bags and hockey sticks, and push air through a pipe of milk tins.
8. But every bag of sand is bright yellow, and the ground up top is grey.
9. So you hide it in your trousers, and shake it out in the gardens.
10. Three hundred feet later, you break through, past the fence.
11. But you're short of the trees, right next to a guard tower.
12. Seventy-six men crawled out that night. Only three made it home.
13. This was the Great Escape.

## Scene idea

Hook: night, searchlight sweeping a barbed-wire fence, a wooden hut; the ground is sliced open like a cake under the camp.
Microphones are small boxes in the soil by the fence with sound rings. The shaft drops under the hut's stove; a tiny
figure on a trolley. Yellow sand under grey topsoil is the visual idea of beat 8. A long tunnel runs left to right under
the fence; the exit pops up in the open, the trees a few metres too far, a guard tower close by.
